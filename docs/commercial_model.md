# Commercial offer — proposal, not an SLA or pilot result

Positioning: give agents authorized operations instead of provider keys. Complement
existing secret managers, API gateways and provider-native idempotency; do not
claim to replace all of them or eliminate prompt injection.

## Offer

1. Paid fixed-scope design-partner pilot: one tenant, one agent, one sandbox provider
   operation, defined deliverables/acceptance, operator training and final evidence.
2. Annual supported enterprise distribution: a defined production environment and
   included tenant allowance, supported version/upgrade path and agreed maintenance.
   Avoid charging per denied call or replica: protection and HA should not be penalized.
3. Separately scoped integration/operations services. No exclusive core license fee
   is implied: the published core remains MIT. Paid differentiators and third-party
   licenses require explicit agreements; do not retroactively restrict MIT grants.

Do not offer 24/7 response, guaranteed recovery time or risk transfer until staffing,
escalation drills, liability terms and customer responsibilities are agreed. Security
report acknowledgement targets in SECURITY.md are not incident-response SLA promises.
Define support hours, severity, response vs resolution, supported versions, maintenance,
customer-owned backups/Redis, provider outages and EOL notice in the signed order.

## Evidence-based business case

Baseline engineering time for secret integration/rotation, incident reconciliation,
installation and maintenance; compare the same workload after integration. Include
license, infrastructure, on-call and uncertainty resolution costs. Label estimated
incident avoidance separately from observed savings. Do not infer ROI from test
counts, claim zero update cost, or count existing provider deduplication twice.

Pitch: (1) scoped agent credential/authority risk, (2) policy-bounded broker path,
(3) exact-commit evidence and explicit limits, (4) paid pilot with measurable success.
GitHub Releases/private registries are initial delivery channels; marketplaces are
later procurement channels, not the product itself. No broad SaaS/control-plane
expansion before the first repeatable customer workflow is proven.

## Customer acceptance and promotion

Pilot agreement identifies data classification/retention, service account ownership,
deployment topology, workload targets, failure drills, evidence reviewers and exit
criteria. No real customer outcome, independent certificate, compliance status or
financial guarantee is asserted by this template. Procurement/security review and
commercial terms must be approved by the responsible parties before production.
